SPHEREx: Mapping the Cosmos in 102 Colors

Spectro-Photometer for the History of the Universe, Epoch of Reionization, and Ices Explorer

SPHEREx is an all-sky near-infrared space observatory conducting the first-ever all-sky near-infrared spectral survey. While flagship observatories like JWST function as cosmic microscopes—zooming into narrow target fields in extreme detail—SPHEREx functions as a wide-angle panoramic camera. Scanning the entire celestial sphere every six months, it maps hundreds of millions of galaxies and stars across 102 contiguous infrared wavelength bands.

Core Scientific Objectives

Three Primary Science Pillars

Pillar 1: Cosmic Inflation
Probing the Origin of the Universe

Investigates the first fraction of a second (< 10⁻³² s) after the Big Bang during exponential cosmic inflation. By cataloging 3D positions and redshifts of > 450M galaxies, SPHEREx maps matter distributions to isolate fossil quantum fluctuations.

3D Cosmological Large-Scale Structure
Pillar 2: Galaxy Formation History
Tracing Origin of Cosmic Light

Measures the total integrated extragalactic background light and intra-halo light across 13 billion years. Quantifies collective photon output from faint, early stellar populations during the Epoch of Reionization that cannot be resolved individually.

Extragalactic Background & Intra-Halo Glow
Pillar 3: Interstellar Ices
Building Blocks of Habitable Worlds

Probes cold dense interstellar clouds and young stellar nurseries across the Milky Way. Using infrared absorption spectroscopy, it measures abundances of frozen water (H₂O), carbon dioxide (CO₂), carbon monoxide (CO), and methanol (CH₃OH) on cosmic dust grains.

Pre-Planetary Volatiles & Biogenic Ices
Hardware & Instrument Blueprint

Single Integrated Instrument Architecture

A. Primary Telescope & Optics 20 cm Aperture

Off-axis, all-aluminum wide-field reflecting telescope ($20\text{ cm} / 7.9\text{ in}$). Compact design optimized for rapid slewing across a massive $3.5^\circ \times 11.3^\circ$ field of view (~20 times wider than the full Moon) spanning $0.75\text{ to }5.0\ \mu\text{m}$.

Field of View: 3.5° × 11.3° All-Aluminum Reflecting Optics
B. Linear Variable Filters (LVF) Zero Moving Parts

Continuous dielectric coatings mounted directly above the detectors vary transmission wavelength along each pixel row. As the telescope sweeps the sky, every astronomical source passes across all rows, constructing complete 102-color spectra with zero moving filter wheels.

102 Spectral Channels Dielectric Thickness Gradient
C. Focal Plane Detector Arrays 6 Teledyne H2RG Arrays

Two $1 \times 3$ mosaics of Teledyne H2RG Mercury-Cadmium-Telluride (HgCdTe) infrared detectors (6 arrays total). Delivers $6.2\text{ arcsec}$ spatial sampling with resolving power $R = \lambda/\Delta\lambda \approx 41$ (short wavelengths) to $R \approx 135$ (long wavelengths).

Pixel Scale: 6.2″ / pixel Resolving Power: R ~ 41–135
D. Passive Cryogenic V-Groove Shields T < 80 K (-193°C)

Three nested cone-shaped aluminum radiative photon shields dump thermal infrared emission directly into deep space. Passively cools the telescope and detector assembly below $80\text{ K}$ without consumable liquid helium cryogens, guaranteeing mission lifetime.

Radiative Deep Space Sink Zero Expendable Cryogens
